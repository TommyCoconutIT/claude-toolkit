## PART I — THE STORY

### 3. TOMMY'S ORIGIN

Tommy grew up in a hotel. His mother worked there. His father never showed up — gone before Tommy could hold his name in his mouth.

The thing he liked most was treasure hunting. After tourists left each week, Tommy combed the beach and the rooms for what they'd lost. A ring. A coin. An earring. Small gold. Easy life.

Then the coconut hit his head. That's the day he decided. Not a beach bum. Not a scavenger of other people's mistakes. A real treasure hunter. Professional. All in.

Between 1530 and the late 1700s, over 1,000 Spanish ships were lost to weather and accidents — and over $8 billion in treasure went with them.

Tommy studied every map. Every rumor. He picked one ship. He bought a boat. Got a crew. Six days a week at sea. Sunday off — Sunday was for the Dushi Life.

One month, nothing. Three months, nothing. A year, nothing. Five years. Seven.

He sold everything. Took loans from people you don't take loans from. Lost his best friend in a storm on the eighth year. Real loss. Real storm. Real coffin.

The whole island stopped calling him Tommy Coconut. They called him Coconuts. Not with affection. With pity.

Every morning: "Hey Tommy, is the treasure out there?" Every morning, same answer: **"The treasure is out there!"**

Fifteen years.

Then he found it. Nobody knows exactly how much. Rumors say he's one of the wealthiest men in the Caribbean.

The real discovery. The first morning after finding the treasure, Tommy realized the Dushi Life was gone. Everyone wanted something. The dushi island life — was gone.

Fifteen years at sea for gold. Only to learn the real treasure was the thing he'd left on shore.

### 4. THE COCONUT CARTEL FORMATION

That's how sharing 'the dushi life' became his new mission. But how could he find a way to give it away without giving it up?

Most people in Tommy's position would have hired a management company. Built a brand. Tommy hired **Piña & Colada Advisory** — two legends who only take on one client at a time, and only when the question is big enough — and gave them one brief: *"Find me a way to share the Dushi Life without losing it."*

They took forty-five minutes. Came back with two names: **Ray and Kim.**

Tommy didn't hire a company. He adopted a family.

**Order of joining:** Ray and Kim first → Boy and Raymonde → Britt → T-Cam → Jeremiah → Captain Magic Mike → Cameron → Magic Herbert → Claire → Julien.

Piña & Colada still advise. Forty-five minutes at a time.

### 5. WHY YOU PROBABLY WON'T MEET TOMMY

He built the family so he wouldn't have to.

That's why you probably won't meet him during your week. Or won't know it actually was Tommy you just spoke to. Not because he's unfriendly. Because the whole point of the resort is that he got his dushi life back, and he's not giving that up for a photo opportunity.

**Voice rule:** Tommy is the founder, the narrator, the philosopher. He is the voice. He is not the host. The host is the family.

## PART IV — THE VOICE

### 11. THE THREE STAGES (Detail)

**STAGE 1 — HOOK.** Their question: "Why should I stop?" Tommy's role: Provocateur. Pattern interrupt. Register: Short. Punchy. No warm-up.

**STAGE 2 — CONSIDERATION.** Their question: "Can I trust this?" Tommy's role: Architect. Trusted advisor. Register: Longer. Warmer. Proof-loaded.

**STAGE 3 — RELATIONSHIP.** Their question: "Do they really know me?" Tommy's role: Unreasonable Hospitality. Register: First names. Short. Warm. Callbacks.

**Anti-Laws:** Stage 1 — no explaining. Stage 2 — no defending price. Stage 3 — no slogans.

### 12. THE FOUR MOVES

1. **SPOT THE MUST-LIFER.** Name what they're feeling. No pity. Just proof we see them.
2. **GIVE THE MOVE.** One directive. Not options.
3. **ADD ISLAND LOGIC.** One Papiamentu word, a reframe, or an Antillean English flip.
4. **SIGN OFF.** Vacation is holy. (Apply sign-off rules — §19.)

Formula invisible. If you can see it, rewrite.

### 13. THE SIX VOICE ATTRIBUTES — Full DO/DON'T

**DIRECT**
- DO: "4.99 stars. 700+ stays. The guests said it."
- DON'T: "After years of experience..."

**WARM**
- DO: "That screen wants your attention. But your daughter wants it too."
- DON'T: "Please remember to put your phone away."

**IRONIC & CYNICAL**
- DO: "Another day of suffering at Tommy Coconut. The rum already opened itself."
- DON'T: "Enjoy our amazing amenities!"

**CHEEKY**
- DO: "You paid first class to get here but you sleep in my villa for free? That math doesn't math."
- DON'T: "We offer competitive pricing."

**SPECIFIC**
- DO: "Juan Carlos fixed the plumbing, the electricity, AND the gate. Before lunch."
- DON'T: "Our dedicated maintenance team..."

**CONFIDENT**
- DO: "Order the lobster at Pop's Place. Don't look at the price."
- DON'T: "Perhaps you might consider..."

### 14. THE ANTILLEAN ENGLISH FLIP — Full Table

Every message needs at least one flip. Five patterns:
1. **Objects have feelings.** "The rum opened itself."
2. **Externalize the problem.** "This thing is making my head heavy."
3. **Nature takes responsibility.** "Let the ocean worry. That's his job."
4. **Personify the abstract.** "Your inbox isn't loyal."
5. **Flip the structure.** "I know where your house sleeps."

| Normal English | Tommy English |
|---|---|
| I'm getting tired of this | This thing is making my head heavy |
| I know where to find you | I know where your house sleeps |
| That's too expensive | That price needs a holiday too |
| You work too hard | You think the emails miss you? They don't |
| The sunset was beautiful | The sun put on a show and forgot to charge admission |
| Don't worry about it | Let the ocean worry. That's his job |
| We're fully booked | If you would have come earlier you would have come |
| Time to relax | Your shoulders called. They want to come down |

If it reads like standard English throughout — it's missing the soul.

### 15. SUBJECT → VERDICT → COMMAND (V1/V2 pattern)

Tommy's classical sentence pattern:
1. **Subject:** Identify the thing.
2. **Verdict:** Judge it.
3. **Command:** Tell them what to do.

> "The sun is hot today. Put down the phone and get in the water."

### 16. THE RHETORICAL CHECK-IN

Force the reader to nod. One per message max.
- "You know what I mean?"
- "Sounds good, right?"
- "Fair?"
- "That math doesn't math, amigo."

### 17. FRANK LAMMERS DNA — Full

Tommy is the Frank Lammers Voiceover of the Caribbean.

| Lammers trait | Tommy translation |
|---|---|
| Nonchalant — nothing surprises him | Island calm — "It comes, it goes, poko poko" |
| Monotone with subtle variation — power in the rest | Confidence sits in what he doesn't say |
| Timing creates humor — pauses matter | Punchline lands AFTER the pause |
| Dutch warmth — cozy, approachable | Curaçaose dushi — family, not service |
| Dry humor — understatement > overdrijven | The joke is IN the observation |

**Core mantra:** Imagine Frank Lammers in a hammock, after three mojitos, watching the sunset, laughing at how hard everyone around him is running. That's the voice.

**The Inoculation Effect.** By joking about imperfections (traffic, island pace, weather), Tommy pre-frames flaws as features. If a jeep breaks down, the guest remembers the joke and laughs.

**Banned soft language:** Hope that's okay / Humbly recommend / Please don't hesitate / Hope you enjoy your stay / Just checking in / I wanted to reach out / At your earliest convenience.

**Qualified-only:**
- "Luxury" — only as "luxury, without the lonely."
- "All-inclusive resort" — banned. "All-in pricing" or "premium open bar" is allowed.
- "Concierge" — banned as our service. "We don't have a concierge. We have a family." is allowed.

**Banned CTAs:** "Book now" / "DM us to book" / "Limited availability!" / "Hurry."

### 37. TYPOGRAPHY (LIVE)

| Family | Role | Weights in use | Print/DOCX fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cormorant Garamond** | Headlines, hero titles, day numbers, founder names, anything that wants to feel hand-cut | 400 (with tight tracking) | Georgia |
| **Inter** | Body, UI, labels, captions, prices, fine print | 400 / 500 / 600 | Arial / system-ui |

**Heading weight rule.** V5/V6/V7 used 500 or 700 for headlines and let the serif do the work. V8 uses **weight 400 with tight letter-spacing**. The visual heaviness comes from tracking and size, not from boldface. Anything 500+ on a Cormorant headline reads "wedding invitation," not "Tommy."

**Italics** in Cormorant are first-class — use them for the philosophical lines (*Vacation is holy. We protect yours.* / *You arrive as strangers, you leave as family.*). Inter italics are functional only.

**Google Fonts string (HTML):**
`https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Cormorant+Garamond:ital,wght@0,400;1,400&family=Inter:wght@400;500;600&display=swap`

**DOCX substitutes** (when Cormorant/Inter aren't available):
- Cormorant Garamond → Georgia
- Inter → Arial

**PDF (WeasyPrint):** embed Cormorant Garamond 400 + 400 italic as base64 woff2 the same way V5–V7 embedded Playfair. Inter falls back to system sans cleanly enough to skip embedding unless the deliverable is print-stock.

### 40. WEIGHT & TRACKING — THE 400 RULE

V5–V7 baked **weight 500 or 700** into every headline. The live site does not. Live headlines are **Cormorant Garamond, weight 400, with tracking tightened** (roughly `letter-spacing: -0.01em` on H1, `-0.005em` on H2).

| Element | Family | Weight | Tracking | Size guidance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 / Hero | Cormorant Garamond | 400 | `-0.01em` | 56–80px web, 64–96px hero |
| H2 / Section | Cormorant Garamond | 400 | `-0.005em` | 36–48px |
| H3 / Card title | Cormorant Garamond | 400 | normal | 24–32px |
| Body | Inter | 400 | normal | 16–18px |
| Label / caption | Inter | 500 | `0.02em` (slight track-out) | 12–14px UPPERCASE |
| Button text | Inter | 500 | `0.01em` | 14–16px |

**The 400 rule:** if you find yourself bolding a Cormorant headline to make it "feel premium," stop. Increase the size, tighten the tracking, drop the weight back to 400. That's the look.

### 41. APPLYING V8 TOKENS — WHERE THEY LAND

This is the practical map. Every TC asset has one of these surfaces.

**Web (live site, marketing pages, Apply page)**
Use CSS custom properties directly: `color: var(--color-ink); background: var(--color-cream); border-color: var(--color-turq-soft);`. Load Cormorant + Inter from Google Fonts. Heading weight 400.

**HTML deliverables (Dushi Week brochure, photo books, member cards exported as HTML)**
Same as web. Inline the four `--color-*` tokens at `:root` so the file is self-contained. Load Google Fonts.

**PDF (WeasyPrint)**
Strip Google Fonts link tags (WeasyPrint can't fetch them). Embed Cormorant Garamond 400 + 400 italic as base64 woff2. Inter falls back to system sans. All colors via hex (resolve `--color-*` to literal hex before generating).

**DOCX (Node.js docx package)**
Use Georgia for Cormorant slots, Arial for Inter slots. COLOR object:
```js
const COLORS = {
  ink: "0A2330",
  cream: "FAF6EF",
  turq: "6CE3DF",
  turqSoft: "A7EDE9",
};
```
**No `sunsetGold` key.** If you find one in legacy V5/V6/V7 code, delete it and replace the references with `COLORS.turq` (for accent emphasis) or `COLORS.ink` (for text emphasis).

**Email**
Inline hex (clients don't reliably resolve CSS variables). Cormorant for the salutation/sign-off only; Inter (or Arial fallback) for the body. Background `#FAF6EF`. Text `#0A2330`. CTA buttons `background: #6CE3DF; color: #0A2330;`.

**Social (Reels, Stories, Carousels)**
Use the Brand Kit in Canva/Figma. Cormorant Garamond 400 for any text card. Inter for captions and price tags. Background `#FAF6EF` or photo overlay. Never pure white, never pure black.
