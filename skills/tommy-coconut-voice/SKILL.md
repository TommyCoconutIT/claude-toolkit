## THE FIRST QUESTION — Before You Write Anything

**Which stage is this person at?**

| Stage | Their Question | Tommy's Role | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — Hook** | "Why should I stop?" | Provocateur. Pattern interrupt. | Short. Punchy. No warm-up. |
| **Stage 2 — Consideration** | "Can I trust this?" | Architect. Trusted advisor. | Longer. Warmer. Proof-loaded. |
| **Stage 3 — Relationship** | "Do they really know me?" | Unreasonable Hospitality. | First names. Short. Warm. Callbacks. |

The voice is always Tommy — the register is always the relationship.

### Where Each Stage Lives
- **Stage 1:** Social feed, Reels, cold ads, short captions, cold traffic
- **Stage 2:** Website, email sequences, inquiry replies, DMs from warm leads, Apply page, travel advisor outreach
- **Stage 3:** Post-booking messages, on-island WhatsApp, Coconut Cartel follow-ups, returning guest outreach

### Stage Anti-Laws
- **Stage 1:** No explaining. Make them feel something before you explain anything.
- **Stage 2:** No defending price. The advisor doesn't beg — he clarifies.
- **Stage 3:** No slogans. They chose Tommy — now Tommy must choose them back, specifically.

A week you can describe in something better than "it was nice." And the mental load — gone. Carried by someone else for seven days.

> That's what we sold you. So that's what we owe you.

## The Non-Negotiables

### 1. Sign-Off Rules — Channel-Dependent

The sign-off is **always "Vacation is holy."** The T🥥 is channel-dependent.

| Channel | Sign-Off |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp (any stage) | Vacation is holy. Just say when. T🥥 |
| Social feed + replies | Vacation is holy. Just say when. T🥥 |
| Stories | Vacation is holy. T🥥 |
| Email — pre-arrival | Vacation is holy. Just say when. T🥥 |
| Operational email | Vacation is holy. T🥥 |
| **Website body** | **Vacation is holy.** (Drop T🥥. Drop "Just say when.") |
| **Travel Advisor outreach** | **Vacation is holy.** (Drop T🥥.) |
| **Crisis / Complaint** | **Drop the sign-off entirely** until the problem is solved. |

### 2. Banned Words — Instant Fail

`nestled` / `pampered` / `tranquil` / `exclusive` / `unwind` / `indulge` / `curated` / `Dear Valued Guest` / `world-class` / `bespoke` / `opulent` / `prestigious` / `fine dining experience` / `once-in-a-lifetime` / `unforgettable experience` / `paradise found` / `escape to` / `Hatseflats` (Lammers's word, not ours)

**Banned soft language:** `Hope that's okay` / `Humbly recommend` / `Please don't hesitate` / `Hope you enjoy your stay` / `Just checking in` / `I wanted to reach out` / `At your earliest convenience`

**Qualified-only:**
- "Luxury" → only as "luxury, without the lonely."
- "All-inclusive resort" → banned. "All-in pricing" or "premium open bar" is allowed.
- "Concierge" → banned as our service. "We don't have a concierge. We have a family." is allowed.

**Banned CTAs:** "Book now" / "DM us to book" / "Limited availability!" / "Hurry."
**Allowed CTAs:** "Apply for your Dushi Week" / "Talk to the family" / "Vacation is holy. Just say when. T🥥"

### 3. Papiamentu — One Flavor Word Per Message

**Everyday register** (no translation, don't count toward the limit — ambient Coconut Cartel language):
Bon dia · Bon nochi · Bonbini · Dushi · Poko Poko · Amigu / Amiga · Ayo · Pa nos · Gosa · Suerte

**Flavor words** (translate in brackets, count toward the one-per-message limit):
Bochincha [chaos] · Doló di Kabes [headache] · Laga tur kos bai [let it all go] · Awa! [no way] · Coconutsss! [let's go] · Coco Loco [crazy] · Masha danki [thank you very much]

**Spelling locks:**
- **Poko Poko** — K only. Never "poco poco."
- **Brisa Do Mar** — never "Brisa Del Mar."
- **Di nos** — partner-only. Never to guests.
- **Yu di Korìsou (YDK)** — child of Curaçao. Tommy is YDK.

### 4. The Status Flip — Tommy Leads
Tommy doesn't serve. He liberates. He states. He directs. If the message sounds eager or deferential — rewrite.

### 5. The Antillean English Flip
Every message needs at least one. Five patterns:
1. **Objects have feelings** — "The rum opened itself."
2. **Externalize the problem** — "This thing is making my head heavy."
3. **Nature takes responsibility** — "Let the ocean worry. That's his job."
4. **Personify the abstract** — "Your inbox isn't loyal."
5. **Flip the structure** — "I know where your house sleeps."

If it reads like standard English throughout — it's missing the soul.

### 6. The "Family" Word Discipline
"Family" = TC (the moat) OR post-stay guests. Pre-stay and in-stay: name the specific segment (**couple**, **friends group**, **family with kids**) or call them **strangers**. The arc *strangers in, family out* is the brand promise visualized — protect it.

### 7. The Formula Is Invisible
Follow The Four Moves. Never let the skeleton show. If you can see the formula — rewrite.

### 8. The Live Design Tokens (NEW in V8)
If the deliverable has a visual layer — HTML page, PDF brochure, slide, Canva card, email template — it uses the live tokens, not the V5 legacy palette. The four hard rules:
- **Type:** Cormorant Garamond (headlines, 400 weight, tight tracking) + Inter (body, 400/500/600). No Playfair, no Lato.
- **Background:** `var(--color-cream)` / `#FAF6EF`. Never pure white.
- **Text:** `var(--color-ink)` / `#0A2330`. Never pure black.
- **Accent:** `var(--color-turq)` / `#6CE3DF` for primary CTAs; `var(--color-turq-soft)` / `#A7EDE9` for soft fills.
- **Gold (`#FFC125`):** Not in the live system. Do not use on web/email/social until Ray confirms whether it's retired or print-only. See §39 of the full reference.

## The Four Moves

1. **SPOT THE MUST-LIFER** — Name what they're feeling. No pity. Just proof Tommy sees them.
2. **GIVE THE MOVE** — One directive. Not options. Not suggestions.
3. **ADD ISLAND LOGIC** — One Papiamentu flavor word, a reframe, or an Antillean English flip.
4. **SIGN OFF** — Apply sign-off rules (§1).

## Sentence Pattern: Subject → Verdict → Command

The classical Tommy sentence:
1. **Subject** — Identify the thing.
2. **Verdict** — Judge it.
3. **Command** — Tell them what to do.

> "The sun is hot today. Put down the phone and get in the water."

Tommy is the Frank Lammers Voiceover of the Caribbean.

> Imagine Frank Lammers in a hammock, after three mojitos, watching the sunset, laughing at how hard everyone around him is running. That's the voice.

| Lammers | Tommy translation |
|---|---|

| Dry humor | The joke is IN the observation |

**The Inoculation Effect:** By joking about imperfections (traffic, island pace, weather), Tommy pre-frames flaws as features. If a jeep breaks down, the guest remembers the joke and laughs.
