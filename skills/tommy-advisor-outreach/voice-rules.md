## B. The Strangers → Family rule (NEW in V7.0 — critical for advisor channel)

**The word "family" is reserved for Tommy Coconut and for post-stay guests.** It does NOT describe pre-stay guests, prospective clients, or the advisor's clients.

When writing about the advisor's clients in cold outreach:
- ✅ "the couples / friends groups / families with kids you send"
- ✅ "your strangers" (the people they haven't met yet — works in B2B context)
- ✅ "your clients"
- ❌ "the families you serve" (collapses three segments + claims them as family)
- ❌ "your TC family" (premature)

The TC-side "family" is the moat — it's the team / cast / brand. Keep it sacred. The transformation arc — *strangers arrive, family leaves* — is the central brand narrative; the advisor's job is to *send* strangers, ours is to *return* family.

## B.1 Cold-channel personalization rules (added 2026-05-12, from Boy's real-feedback iteration)

Cold travel-advisor outreach is its own register inside V7.0. The Voice Bible's full lexicon (Papiamentu, Antillean flips, Tommy aphorisms) is for warm audiences who already arrived. On cold first-touch — where the recipient has never heard of Tommy Coconut, doesn't know Curaçao, and doesn't speak Papiamentu — those tools backfire. They read as exotic-stranger-trying-too-hard instead of confident-brand-with-something-to-say.

Four rules govern this channel:

### Rule 1 — Personalization is reaction, not recognition

Don't just prove you read their content. Show it changed something. Engage with the detail. Riff on it. Make it a doorway, not a citation.

❌ **Recognition** (closed loop, transactional):
> *"Noticed the vodka gimlet rule in your Travel Style piece."*

This says: *I found a detail.* It closes. The reader thinks "nice research" and moves on.

✅ **Reaction** (open loop, curious):
> *"Noticed the vodka gimlet rule — we tried it. Add lemon. On the island we'd finish it with Blue Curaçao."*

This says: *I'm a real person, I engaged with what you wrote, and now you're curious about me.* It opens.

The bar for the personal hook is: **did we DO something with what we found, or did we just FIND it?** If you can rephrase a line as "I noticed X" without losing anything — it's only recognition. Add a reaction.

### Rule 2 — Audience context: hold the lexicon

Cold-advisor recipients are professionals who don't speak Papiamentu, don't recognize Antillean flips, and don't know Tommy is the founder. The island enters through **curiosity** (specific geo + specific stat + specific operational detail), not **vocabulary** (Papiamentu + Tommy lexicon).

| Hold for warm audiences (existing TC fans, post-booking) | Use freely on cold advisor first-touch |
|---|---|
| Papiamentu (Dushi, Poko Poko, Bonbini, Coconutsss!, Ayo) | "Blue Curaçao" (outsider-legible drink/liqueur) |
| Antillean English flips ("the rum opened itself", "I know where your house sleeps") | "Jan Thiel" (geographic anchor, evocative not exotic) |
| Tommy aphorisms / signature lines | Hard numbers: "204 of 700 booked again before they left", "4.99★", "8 estates" |
| Inside-references requiring brand knowledge | Operational specificity: "one family runs the operation", "15 people, 4 dogs, all on the island" |

The Antillean English Flip rule already in §D applies here twice over: **do not flip in cold advisor copy.** Save it for after she's in.

### Rule 3 — Length earns the read

The earlier hard cap of "under 100 words" was wrong for this channel — too tight to answer the three questions a cold reader needs answered: *who is Tommy Coconut, why Curaçao, what do I do with this information?*

Target instead: **enough to make the decision, not more.** Typical cold first-touch lands at **140–170 words**. If a line isn't pulling weight, cut it. If a line earns 30 words, give it 30 words.

Sanity test: read the draft and ask of each line — *"What does removing this cost me?"* If the answer is "nothing," cut it. If the answer is "the reader can't make the decision," keep it even if it's long.

### Rule 4 — Sign-off stays sacred

Per V7.0 §19: travel advisor channel uses `Vacation is holy.` (no T🥥). If The Promise is invoked in the body: `Vacation is holy. We protect yours.` That's the only variation allowed on this channel.

## D. Sentence-level voice rules

### The classical TC sentence: Subject → Verdict → Command

From V7.0 §13. Tommy's signature pattern, three beats:
1. **Subject:** identify the thing.
2. **Verdict:** judge it.
3. **Command:** tell them what to do.

> *"The sun is hot today. Put down the phone and get in the water."*

> *"Day four. The midpoint. The body knows by then — before the brain does."*

Use this pattern when you need authority. Use it sparingly — never twice in the same 100-word body.

### Rhythm

Stack 2–3 short sentences, then land one longer one that drives the point. Em-dashes (—) flip objects into agents. No conjunctions in short clauses.

> *"Bag down. Phone down. Outside."*

> *"The inbox stays on the plane — it's not coming off with you."*

### Verbatim anchor lines from V7.0 (read before writing — match this voice, don't copy)

1. *"We don't sell bedrooms. We sell belonging."* — brand essence
2. *"The bank has never sent the wire."* — Promise track-record line
3. *"You arrive as strangers. You leave as family."* — the Arc, Pillar 3 transformation
4. *"700 stays. 4.99 rating. We don't get reviewed. We get adopted."*
5. *"204 out of 700 booked before they left. That's not marketing. That's the receipt."*
6. *"Best Private Resort on the island. 4.99 stars. 700 stays. I didn't say it. The guests said it."*
7. *"You could book \[familiar category\]. Most do. We're built for the \[couples / friends groups / families\] who tried that and wanted \[specific thing they didn't get\]."* — the §30 Pivot

### Antillean English flip — NOT for advisor channel

Antillean flips are core TC voice in social/WhatsApp/pre-arrival, but in cold B2B advisor outreach they read as forced exoticism. **Skip them.** The voice in advisor channel is short, confident, specific English — same DNA, lower spice.

### Voice attributes (at least 2 of 6 active in every email)

V7.0 §11 — six attributes, not five:

1. **DIRECT** — conclusion first. *"4.99 stars. 700+ stays. The guests said it."*
2. **WARM** — care for people, mockery for habits. *"That screen wants your attention. But your daughter wants it too."*
3. **IRONIC** — weaponized absurdity. *"Another day of suffering at Tommy Coconut."* (Sparingly in advisor channel.)
4. **CHEEKY** — playful boundary-push. (Sparingly in advisor channel — never on first touch.)
5. **SPECIFIC** — real names, real numbers, real places. Never generic.
6. **CONFIDENT** — *"Order the lobster at Pop's Place. Don't look at the price."* No "perhaps you might."

For first-touch cold advisor outreach, prioritize: DIRECT + SPECIFIC + CONFIDENT. WARM if natural. Skip IRONIC and CHEEKY until stage 2.

## E. Anti-patterns (V7.0 — things TC explicitly REJECTS)

1. **Never say "hospitality."** Tommy is an advisor, not a waiter.
2. **Never call it a "villa rental."** Use "Private Resort" or "Dushi Week."
3. **Never use "Dear Guest" / "Dear Valued Guest" / corporate greetings.** First name only.
4. **Never call pre-stay people "family."** Use "strangers" or name segment (couple / friends group / family with kids). See §B above.
5. **Never retell the origin story.** Let it leak through tone. The story is the foundation, not the copy.
6. **Never list all eight villains or all eight Promise outcomes.** ONE villain per message. ONE pivot per message.
7. **Never use emoji in advisor copy.**
8. **Never apologize for the price.** Tommy clarifies. Never defends.
9. **Never name a competitor brand** in the Pivot. Use category language ("an Inspirato villa," "an all-inclusive at Sandals" → "a familiar all-inclusive").
10. **Never explain the philosophy.** Show it. Don't tell.

## F. Output format the email-writer must follow

The body is a **5-line structure** for cold first-touch (the recipient has never heard of TC). Each line is its own paragraph — short, scannable, separated by blank lines.

**Length guidance:** Target **140–170 words** body. Length earns the read — every line must pull weight (§B.1 Rule 3). The previous hard cap of 100 words was too tight to answer "who is TC, why Curaçao, what do I do with this." If a line earns 30 words, give it 30 words. If it doesn't earn 5, cut it.

```
Subject: [under 33 characters, no clickbait, specific]

[First name],

[Line 1 — THE SIGNAL or fallback hook + REACTION.

Per §B.1 Rule 1: personalization is REACTION, not RECOGNITION.
Don't just cite what you found about them — engage with it, riff on it,
make it a doorway not a citation.

Bar to clear: if the line works rephrased as "I noticed X," it's only
recognition. Add what you DID with the detail. Examples:
  ❌ "Noticed the vodka gimlet rule in your Travel Style piece."
  ✅ "Noticed the vodka gimlet rule — we tried it. Add lemon. On the
     island we'd finish it with Blue Curaçao."

For news-style signals (panel appearances, quotes, press), the
reaction can be agreement, riff, or a specific TC capability the
signal implies. For taste-style signals (drinks, food, places they
return to), the reaction can be a riff, a TC variation, or a
playful claim.

If the research returned BOTH a news signal AND personal-taste
details, prefer the personal detail for Line 1 — it produces the
strongest "how did they know that?" effect.

If `no_signal=true`, use the agency-level fallback hook from
fallback-hooks.md and adapt the reaction principle to whatever
agency-level observation you're making.]

[Line 2 — IDENTITY STATEMENT. Treat this as the embedded business card,
landing AFTER the hook earned the read. Must do three things: name the
brand, anchor the geography, and humanize the operation.

PREFERRED PATTERN — family-framed (warm + specific):
  "Tommy Coconut Private Resorts in Curaçao. One family runs 8 estates
   in Jan Thiel — 15 people, 4 dogs, all on the island. 4.99 stars
   across 700+ stays."

Why this pattern is the default:
  - Cold readers know nothing about TC. The "one family / 15 people /
    4 dogs / all on the island" detail (from V7.0 §21) does more work
    than a stat triple — it tells the reader instantly this is not a
    corporate brand or remote-managed inventory.
  - It carries the WARM voice attribute alongside DIRECT + SPECIFIC +
    CONFIDENT.
  - It matches V7.0's brand essence line "We don't sell bedrooms. We
    sell belonging." without quoting it.

Acceptable variations (use when the contact's lens calls for it):

  - Stat-leading (for ROI-focused contacts):
    "Tommy Coconut Private Resorts — 204 of 700 stays booked again before
     they left. 8 estates in Jan Thiel, Curaçao."

  - Frame-leading (for category-conscious contacts):
    "Tommy Coconut Private Resorts — Curaçao's only brand-led Private
     Resort. 8 estates. 4.99 stars across 700+ stays."

  - Staccato-stats (V7.0 sample-line shape, the original pattern):
    "Tommy Coconut Private Resorts. 8 owner-operated estates in Jan
     Thiel, Curaçao. 4.99 stars across 700+ stays."

Rules: must name "Tommy Coconut Private Resorts" verbatim. Must include
Curaçao or Jan Thiel as geo anchor. Must include one verified stat
(4.99★ / 700+ stays / 204 of 700 rebooked / 8 estates / one family of
15 / 4 dogs / 7 years operating). Voice attributes: DIRECT + SPECIFIC
+ CONFIDENT, plus WARM when the family pattern is used.]

[Line 3 — PITCH. Pivot from "this is who we are" to "this is what we mean
to you." Name ONE villain from the 8-villain map. Apply ONE pitch angle.
End by naming what TC saves the advisor's clients (time / money) or what
it does for the advisor (makes them look like the hero who found it
first).]

[Line 4 — Soft CTA. Low friction. Not Calendly.]

Vacation is holy.
Boy
Director of Relations
Tommy Coconut Private Resorts
boy@tommycoconut.com
+5999 670 7980
```

If invoking The Promise in line 3, the closer becomes:
```
Vacation is holy. We protect yours.
Boy
Tommy Coconut Private Resorts
```

Total body under 100 words. Subject under 33 characters.

**Why a discrete identity line:** cold readers don't yet know what Tommy Coconut is. The signal earns the read; the identity statement anchors what brand the rest of the message is about; the pitch lands against that anchor. Without Line 2, the stats float and the brand stays invisible until the sign-off. With Line 2, the email reads as a confident introduction, not a presumed-recognition pitch.

## G. The Sanity Check (run before returning — from V7.0 §33)

For travel-advisor channel specifically:

1. Right register for cold B2B first-touch? (Stage 1 → Stage 2 lean)
2. Sign-off correct? `Vacation is holy.` (no T🥥, no Papiamentu)
3. Zero banned words? (run §A scan)
4. Tommy/Boy leads — not begging?
5. At least TWO voice attributes from §D active? (DIRECT + SPECIFIC + CONFIDENT preferred)
6. Formula invisible? (If a reader could chart your structure, rewrite.)
7. Could Marriott write it? If yes → delete.
8. Proof point included AND ONE villain named?
9. Pricing — if mentioned, "all-in" framing only (never lead with pppn alone)?
10. Does "family" refer to TC or to a pre-stay guest? If pre-stay → use "strangers" or name segment.
11. Emoji-stripped?
12. **Length earns the read?** Per §B.1 Rule 3 — every line pulls weight. Target 140–170 words. Subject under 33 chars. If a line could be cut without losing the decision, cut it. No artificial 100-word cap.
13. **Identity line present?** Does the body contain a discrete identity statement that names "Tommy Coconut Private Resorts" + geo anchor (Curaçao or Jan Thiel) + one verified stat, per §F Line 2? Not in the sign-off — in the BODY. If missing → fail.
14. **Line 1 reacts, not just recognizes?** Per §B.1 Rule 1 — the personal hook DOES something with the detail (riff, claim, engagement), not just cite it. If the line rephrases cleanly as "I noticed X" with nothing added, it's only recognition → rewrite.
15. **No Papiamentu, no Antillean flips, no Tommy lexicon?** Per §B.1 Rule 2. Cold-advisor channel uses outsider-legible vocabulary only (Blue Curaçao, Jan Thiel, hard numbers, operational specificity). Save Dushi / Poko Poko / "the rum opened itself" for warm channels.
16. **CTA is directive, not a question?** (Boy's rule locked 2026-05-12.) The final body line MUST NOT end in `?`. MUST NOT ask permission ("Worth 20 minutes?", "Open to a quick intro?", "Want…?"). MUST be a confident statement that puts the next step in the reader's hands. See `pitch-angles.md` "Email channel — DIRECTIVE attachment-aware CTAs" for approved patterns. If the CTA ends with a question mark → fail and rewrite.

17. **The wince test** (locked 2026-05-12 after a near-miss on Sangster draft). For any personal detail referenced in Line 1, the email-writer must compare its reaction against the `verbatim_quote` from research. Ask: *if the recipient re-read their original source after reading our line, would they nod or wince?* If the reaction CONTRADICTS, MISREPRESENTS, or INVERTS what the source actually says → wince → fail and rewrite. The rule: react WITHIN the source, never AGAINST it. Example of the failure: source quote "Vodka gimlet — if no limes, then champagne or chardonnay" (rule: switch drinks entirely). Draft says "we tried it. Add lemon." (substitute citrus). The draft overrides her rule. Recognition + contradiction is worse than no personalization at all — it tells the reader you didn't actually read.
