### 3. The "Receipt" angle (works across agency types)

**Core line:** 204 out of 700 booked before they left. That's not marketing — that's the receipt.

**Use when:** the contact's signal suggests they care about retention, repeat clients, building referral pipelines, or how to defend a recommendation.

**Sample fragment:**
> *"4.99 stars. 700 stays. 204 booked their next week before they finished the current one. We don't get reviewed. We get adopted."*

### 6. The Tommy Coconut Promise (V7.0 §6 — THE primary advisor outreach angle per V7.0 §31)

**Core line:** Day 4. The midpoint. If even one of eight wishes doesn't land, full balance back the same day. The bank has never sent the wire.

**Use when:** you want to give the advisor the most operationally confident statement TC makes — a tool they can use against price objections, against trust objections, against "what if it doesn't deliver" objections. **This is the angle V7.0 explicitly directs at advisor outreach.**

**The eight wishes (NEVER list more than ONE in a single email):**
1. A vacation that actually delivers
2. The people they came with, still in the room with each other by Wednesday
3. A real story to tell — not "we did a boat tour"
4. Real island life — not the same big chain three nights running
5. Someone actually on it — not "will be in touch shortly"
6. One all-in price — no checkout statement that grew teeth
7. A week describable in something better than "it was nice"
8. The mental load — gone, carried by someone else for seven days

**Sample fragment:**
> *"Here's what your couples and friends groups don't know yet. Day 4 of every Dushi Week, we run a check. If even one of eight wishes didn't land, the full balance is back in their account the same day. The bank has never sent the wire. That's not marketing — that's the receipt of an operation built around an outcome, not a transaction."*

**Closer when this angle is used:**

## The 8-Villain Map (V7.0 §8 — pick exactly ONE per email)

Each villain is paired 1:1 with a Promise wish. Pick the villain that matches the advisor's specialization or the signal. **Never list more than one. Never name a competitor brand.**

| # | The wish | The villain to name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A vacation that delivers | The disappointing villa rental |
| 2 | Connection with the people they came with | Everyone checking out by Wednesday — kids on phones, spouse on Slack, friends scrolling |
| 3 | Adventure | "We did a boat tour" being the only story they have |
| 4 | Real island life | Eating at the same big chain name three nights running |
| 5 | Trust | "We will be in touch shortly" |
| 6 | Value confirmation | Hidden fees, resort fees, cleaning fees, service charges |
| 7 | A story to tell | "It was nice" being the only review they can give |
| 8 | Relaxation | The mental load of planning everything themselves |

**Segmentation note:** The villains apply equally to couples, friends groups, and families with kids. Villain #2 takes a different specific shape per segment (kids on phones / spouse on Slack / friends scrolling) — pick the shape that matches the advisor's typical client.

## The "Better than" Pivot (V7.0 §30 — explicit advisor template)

When the advisor is comparing TC to a category their clients already know, use this exact template:

> *"You could book \[familiar category\]. Most do. We're built for the \[couples / friends groups / families with kids\] who tried that and wanted \[specific thing they didn't get\]."*

**Rules:**
- No brand names. Use category language. "An Inspirato villa," not "Inspirato." "A familiar all-inclusive," not "Sandals."
- Confident, not wounded.
- Close on the specific thing we deliver — never on what the other thing failed at.
- One Pivot per piece.
- **Vary the segment named in the pivot** — couples one week, friends groups the next, families the next.

**Examples:**
- *"You could book a luxury all-inclusive. Most do. We're built for the friends groups who want to actually leave the property."*
- *"You could DIY a villa rental. Most do. We're built for the couples who tried that once and spent half the week troubleshooting the AC."*
- *"You could put a multi-gen family at a familiar resort. Most do. We're built for the families who wanted three generations at the same table — and someone on WhatsApp on Saturday night."*

## The CTA bank (pick one per email)

Low-friction asks only. Never Calendly. Never "book now."

**Email channel — DIRECTIVE attachment-aware CTAs.** The one-pager is ALWAYS attached when Boy sends, so the CTA must reflect that — never ask for something we're already providing.

**Boy's directive rule (locked 2026-05-12):** CTAs for senior travel advisors are STATEMENTS, not questions. No question mark. No asking permission. No "Worth 20 minutes?" hedging. The reader is a senior professional — the close should be a confident next step or a conditional invitation, not a polite request for their calendar.

**Preferred patterns (directive, no question mark):**
- "One-pager attached. If [their situation — e.g., 'Curaçao isn't in your portfolio yet'], that's where this starts."
- "One-pager attached. Let me know if the numbers make sense for your [network / advisors / clients]."
- "Deck attached. The math is in there — tell me if it works for your model."
- "Pager attached. Read when you have a moment — I'll follow up next week."

**Why this works:** these put the next step in the reader's hands without asking permission to take their time. *"Let me know if the numbers make sense"* is a statement they can act on; *"Worth 20 minutes?"* is a request they have to grant. Senior people respond better to the first.

**Other allowed CTAs (use when natural):**
- "Apply for your own Dushi Week if you want to see it firsthand." (FAM-trip framing — use carefully)
- "Talk to the family." (V7.0 allowed CTA — works when conversational, not in cold first-touch)

**DEPRECATED for email channel — DO NOT USE:**
- ~~"Want the one-pager?"~~ — the pager is already attached.
- ~~"Want me to send the one-pager?"~~ — same reason.
- ~~"Worth 20 minutes?"~~ — asks permission. Hedge.
- ~~"Worth 20 minutes when you've got a quiet hour?"~~ — also incoherent (20 min vs quiet hour).
- ~~"Open to a quick intro?"~~ — asks permission.
- ~~"Two minutes on a call before [season]?"~~ — asks permission.
- ~~"Want a quiet walk-through before your next client asks?"~~ — asks permission.
- ~~"Happy to walk through it on a call if useful"~~ — over-soft hedge.
- Any CTA ending in a question mark on this channel.

## What never goes in an email

- Calendly links
- "Hop on a call" / "Book now" / "Limited availability" / "Hurry"
- The full origin story (let it leak through tone)
- More than one villain in one message
- More than one Promise wish per message
- The 🥥 emoji or any emoji
- Any Papiamentu word (V7.0 §31 — drop for advisor channel)
- The word "hospitality"
- The word "family" applied to pre-stay clients (use "strangers" or named segment)
- Any number beyond the verified stats above
- A competitor's brand name in the Pivot
